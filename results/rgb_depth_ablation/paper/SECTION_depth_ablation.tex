\subsection{Does Depth Explain the Habit's Performance?}
\label{sec:depth_ablation}

The habit policy receives depth, which the OpenVLA-OFT teacher does not. This raises
the question of whether the habit's held-out success reflects habit formation or
simply privileged sensing. We screen this directly. On \Num{AblaClusters} clusters
stratified by suite and difficulty---selected and frozen before any result was
produced---we retrain the habit with the depth channel removed and nothing else
changed. Both conditions consume the same teacher trajectories, the same
$\mathcal{B}_k$ ordering, the same optimizer, schedule, and seed, and are evaluated on
the same \Num{AblaHeldout} held-out episode specifications, so every episode is paired.
Removing depth narrows the first convolution from four channels to three, a difference
of \Num{AblaParamDelta} parameters (\Num{AblaParamPct}\%).

Over \Num{AblaEpisodes} paired episodes the mean difference is \Num{AblaPairedDelta}
with a bootstrap 95\% confidence interval of $[\Num{AblaPairedLo},
\Num{AblaPairedHi}]$, which contains zero; the exact McNemar test on
\Num{AblaDiscRGB}/\Num{AblaDiscRGBD} discordant pairs gives $p = \Num{AblaMcNemarP}$.
Table~\ref{tab:depth_ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:depth_ablation} resolve this by
cluster. The formation threshold $N^{\star}$ is unchanged in \Num{AblaNstarSame} of
\Num{AblaClusters} clusters, and the \Num{AblaNstarMoved} that move do so in opposite
directions, so depth removal does not systematically delay formation. The effect
across $n$ is not monotone: the mean difference is \Num{AblaDeltaN10}~pp at $n=10$,
\Num{AblaDeltaN20}~pp at $n=20$, \Num{AblaDeltaN40}~pp at $n=40$, and
\Num{AblaDeltaN80}~pp at $n=80$. Depth therefore helps where data is scarcest and
contributes little at the ceiling on which the maturity certificate---and hence every
claim in this paper about substituting the habit for the teacher---depends.

Two qualifications are needed. First, the mean drop at $n=80$ is
\Num{AblaDropEighty}~pp, and it is carried almost entirely by the single
long-suite cluster ($\Delta = \Num{AblaLongDelta}$), which is right-censored under
both conditions and never forms at all; excluding it the mean drop is
\Num{AblaDropEightyExLong}~pp. Depth may thus matter more for long-horizon tasks than
this screen can resolve. Second, the cluster we expected to depend most on depth,
a Spatial cluster, shows $\Delta = \Num{AblaSpatialDelta}$ and an unchanged
$N^{\star}$, so a geometric reading of which tasks need depth is not supported. All
failures in both conditions reach the episode step cap rather than terminating early,
which indicates that removing depth degrades completion rather than causing
qualitatively different failures; separating grasp, localization, and placement
failures would require per-episode video and is left to future work. This is a
screening experiment on \Num{AblaClusters} of the 33 clusters, not a formal
equivalence test.
